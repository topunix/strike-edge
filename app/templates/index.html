How it works

Perpetual futures use funding to stay near the index price: when the rate is positive, longs pay shorts; when negative, shorts pay longs. Strike Edge reads live funding, prices and order books from Strike, Hyperliquid and OKX, then subtracts fees and slippage.

Holding cost asks where it is cheapest to hold one long or short. The two-leg strategy goes long on one venue and short on another and asks whether the funding gap beats the costs of four fills.

The Move to Strike? tab is for a position you already hold on Hyperliquid or OKX: it compares keeping it there with closing it and reopening on Strike, after fees, slippage and funding.

Bots and agents: use the API, or connect an AI assistant to the MCP endpoint at /mcp.

FAQ
Why are these only estimates?

Funding rates change, slippage depends on live depth, prices on two venues can drift apart (basis), and maker fills are not guaranteed. We assume current funding persists.

What does confidence mean?

High: fresh data, deep books and a steady funding sign over 24h. Low: stale data, thin books, closed underlying market, ticker-only match or unstable funding. An unverified Hyperliquid fee lowers it one level.

Where does the data come from?

Public Strike, Hyperliquid and OKX APIs, refreshed every 60 seconds. Venue data older than 5 minutes is greyed out and left out of rankings.
